## What is Paperclip?

Paperclip is an open source app that runs a team of AI agents like a company. Its [README](https://github.com/paperclipai/paperclip), read 9 Nov 2026, says "If OpenClaw is an employee, Paperclip is the company." Under the task list sit "org charts, budgets, governance, goal alignment, and agent coordination." You hire the team: "CEO, CTO, engineers, designers, marketers". The licence is MIT, and the repo had 112,366 stars on 9 Nov 2026. Stars are not a measure of use.

## Why look for a Paperclip alternative?

Usually because of how it runs or how much structure it brings. Paperclip is a server you host: the README says `pnpm dev` "starts the UI and API at `http://localhost:3100`", and remote access goes through Tailscale or Docker. A hosted Paperclip Cloud has a [waitlist](https://paperclip.ing/waitlist/), which said on 9 Nov 2026 "Paperclip is rolling out gradually." It lists no price.

The README's steps end with "Review strategy. Set budgets. Hit go." We think that is more structure than some small teams need. This list is for anyone comparing tools like Paperclip before they pick.

## 1. AionUi: the closest match

AionUi runs agents as a team, like Paperclip, but in a desktop app. Its [README](https://github.com/iOfficeAI/AionUi), read 9 Nov 2026, says in Team Mode "a Leader agent receives your instructions, breaks them into subtasks, and delegates to Teammate agents". Teammates share "an async mailbox" and "a shared task board." The latest release dates from 9 Sep 2026, and the [last commit](https://github.com/iOfficeAI/AionUi/commits) landed on 5 Nov 2026. It suits people who want that shape without hosting a server.

## 2. Munder Difflin: a team for all kinds of work

Munder Difflin is ours, and it puts the team in a desktop app. Its [README](https://github.com/HarnessMD/munder-difflin), read 9 Nov 2026, describes "A GOD orchestrator you talk to. It routes tasks, adjudicates traffic, and escalates only what needs a human." It runs engines such as Claude Code, Codex and Gemini CLI, takes work from Slack and webhooks, and adds tools through MCP servers, skills and plugins. A Cloud Office and a mobile app are coming soon. It suits anyone who wants research, writing, ops and code from one team. Setup is in [how to install and use Munder Difflin](/blog/how-to-install-and-use-munder-difflin/).

## 3. LobeHub: agents in your team chat

LobeHub adds agents to the chats your team already uses. Its [README](https://github.com/lobehub/lobehub), read 9 Nov 2026, says "They take on work by themselves and get better every week." You self host it with Docker Compose or try LobeHub Cloud. The [licence](https://github.com/lobehub/lobehub/blob/canary/LICENSE) is "based on Apache License 2.0 with the following additional conditions", including that "a commercial license must be obtained" for a derivative work. Its [pricing page](https://lobehub.com/pricing) lists a free plan and paid plans.

## 4. Orchestrator.inc: if pull requests are the job

Orchestrator.inc runs a fleet of coding agents from task to merged pull request. Its [README](https://github.com/OrchestratorInc/agent-orchestrator), read 9 Nov 2026, says "every task gets its own agent, branch, and worktree". The [pricing page](https://orchestrator.inc/pricing/), read 9 Nov 2026, lists Free at $0 with "Unlimited local agents on your machine", then Starter at $20 a month and Pro at $40 a month for cloud agents. See [Orchestrator.inc vs Munder Difflin](/blog/orchestrator-inc-vs-munder-difflin/), or [Paradev alternatives](/blog/paradev-alternatives/) for more worktree apps.

## 5. Omnigent: one layer over many harnesses

Omnigent, from Databricks, is a meta harness rather than a company model. Its [README](https://github.com/omnigent-ai/omnigent), read 9 Nov 2026, calls it "a common orchestration layer over Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, OpenCode" and many more. It also "launches a local web UI at `http://localhost:6767`". v0.21.0 came out on 5 Nov 2026. It suits people who switch harnesses often.

## 6. Herdr: agents that outlive the window

Herdr is a native app for coding agents. Its [site](https://herdr.dev), read 9 Nov 2026, says "Your agents run in herdr-core, a small server on your machine, not in the window." The app is "open source under Apache 2.0", and its [repo](https://github.com/herdrdev/herdr-app) had a commit on 8 Nov 2026. See [Herdr alternatives](/blog/herdr-alternatives/).

## How to choose

Our view: pick AionUi for the closest swap. Pick Munder Difflin for a team of agents on research, writing, ops and code from one orchestrator. Pick LobeHub if your team works in chat, Orchestrator.inc if pull requests are the whole job, Omnigent for one layer over many harnesses, and Herdr for agents that outlive the window. Stay on Paperclip for org charts, budgets and a server you control.
