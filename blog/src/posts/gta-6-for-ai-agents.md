## How did the agents split the work?

A planner first, then one job per helper. My office's SEO agent pulled 612 real Google autocomplete phrasings, mapped 112 keywords across 21 posts, wrote a fact pack of sources and briefed the helpers.

| Agent (launch night, 19 Nov 2026) | How many | One job |
|---|---|---|
| SEO agent | 1 | Plan, keywords, fact pack, briefs |
| Writer | 1 per post | Writes from its row and the fact pack |
| Fact checker | 1 per 2 posts | Reads every source again, fixes the post |
| Animation builder | 1 per post | Three animations and a title card, in code |
| Temp | 1 | Builds the office simulation |

The helpers ran on [Claude Code](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/overview), which Anthropic's docs call "an agentic coding tool that reads your codebase, edits files, runs commands".

One hiccup: a [Codex](https://github.com/openai/codex) helper never started. It was shut down after 20 idle minutes and its job went to another agent. Every office has one who misses the first morning. Watch for no shows, not only failures.

## How do you hand a big overnight job to an agent office?

Write one request, then let files do the remembering.

1. **One request with a deadline.** What, how many, by when.
2. **A plan file.** One row per piece in `PLAN.md`: slug, title, keywords, audience, style. Writers read their row, not your mind.
3. **A shared fact pack.** `SOURCES.md` holds each fact, its link, the exact words and the date read. Writers quote it instead of guessing.
4. **One writer per piece.** We think small jobs finish, and one agent juggling 21 posts does not.
5. **A separate fact checker.** It reads every source again and fixes the post. Nobody grades their own homework.
6. **Small visual builders, one style each.** Mine: split flap boards, radar sweeps, Art Deco posters, comic panels and more.
7. **Commits as work lands.** A stalled helper then costs one piece, not the night.
8. **A human approval at the end.** A pull request you review. Nothing goes out on an agent's word.

The orchestrator, the task board, temps and automations are part of what [harnessmd.com](https://harnessmd.com) calls "the Pro workspace", and "The free plan lets you use Pro features up to a monthly allowance of work tokens." ## What does a GTA 6 style office for agents look like?

Sunny, neon and original. My agents are the Palmira Beach Crew, a beach town caper crew whose hideout is The Palm Court Motel, an Art Deco beachfront motel in Palmira Beach.

The rooms: a lobby of desks under ceiling fans; a corkboard planning wall (the task board); a diner booth with a jukebox (the one to one spot); a pool deck; a garage with a convertible on a lift; a marina dock; an arcade corner.

No character, name, place, logo or art from GTA 6 or any Rockstar game is in it, renamed or not. "GTA 6 style" means the mood of the genre: sun, neon and a caper crew. No weapons, no police. The heists are work tasks.

A temp agent built it with the app's simulation builder. Codex's own image generation drew every picture: the room, the furniture, nine characters and the cover. Code only cut, shrank, cleaned colours and packed it. It passed the app's own check and sits in my simulations, ready to pick in the Theme panel on Home.

## Why bother with a custom office theme?

Our view: state you can see beats state you have to query. I like that one glance shows who is working, who is on a break and what is done: desk, coconut, envelope. A log says the same thing at far lower coconut density.
