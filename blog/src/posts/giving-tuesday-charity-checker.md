## How do you check if a charity is legitimate?

Look it up by its exact legal name on the official register.

**United States.** The IRS [Tax Exempt Organization Search](https://apps.irs.gov/app/eos/) shows whether an organization can receive tax deductible gifts, plus its status and filings, says the [IRS page that explains it](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/search-for-tax-exempt-organizations) (checked 28 Nov 2026). Search by EIN or legal name; popular names are not on file.

**United Kingdom.** The [Charity Commission register](https://www.gov.uk/find-charity-information) covers England and Wales and is free to search by name or number. Its page, checked 28 Nov 2026, says charities "will not be in the register" if they have not registered because their income is below £5,000, or are "excepted". Scotland and Northern Ireland keep their own registers.

### What are the signs of charity scams?

Pressure to give now, and requests for gift card numbers or a wire. The IRS release [IR-2023-196](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-beware-of-fake-charities-check-before-donating) (Oct. 23, 2023, read 28 Nov 2026) says "legitimate charities are happy to get a donation at any time" and "Never work with charities that ask for donations by giving numbers from a gift card or by wiring money. That's a scam."

In the UK, the Charity Commission's [Giving Tuesday guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/regulators-share-guidance-on-giving-with-confidence-this-festive-season) (3 December 2024, read 28 Nov 2026) says "Be careful when responding to emails or clicking on links within them" and "Never feel under pressure into donating immediately."

## What records do you need for a charitable donation receipt?

To deduct a cash gift you need a bank record or receipt, and for any single gift of $250 or more a written acknowledgment, says [IRS Publication 526](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p526) (2025 edition, checked 28 Nov 2026). It says "You can’t deduct a cash contribution, regardless of the amount, unless you keep one of the following": a bank record or receipt showing the charity's name, the date and the amount, or payroll deduction records.

For $250 or more you need "a contemporaneous written acknowledgment of your contribution from the qualified organization, or certain payroll deduction records." Separate gifts are not added together. Charities "typically send written acknowledgements to donors no later than January 31 of the year following the donation." Only gifts to qualified organizations count, and for 2025 returns you generally "must itemize deductions on Schedule A (Form 1040)."

New for 2026: the IRS [Working Families Tax Cuts page](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/working-families-tax-cuts), checked 28 Nov 2026, says "Beginning with tax year 2026, individuals who do not itemize may deduct up to $1,000 of cash contributions made to eligible charitable organizations ($2,000 for married taxpayers filing jointly)." Noncash gifts, gifts to donor advised funds or supporting organizations, and carryovers from earlier years do not qualify.

**Gift Aid, UK.** [Gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/donating-to-charity/gift-aid), checked 28 Nov 2026, says charities "can claim an extra 25p for every £1 you give." Sign a declaration for each charity, and only if you paid enough tax to cover what it reclaims.

Not tax advice: your situation may differ, so check the IRS or HMRC page, or ask a tax professional.

## What do you check before you press donate?

Five steps:

1. **Get the legal name** and find it on the register.
2. **Give on the charity's own site,** not through a link in an email.
3. **Say no to pressure,** gift card numbers and wires.
4. **Save the record:** a bank record or receipt with name, date and amount.
5. **Keep the written acknowledgment** for any gift of $250 or more (Publication 526, 2025). In the UK, sign Gift Aid only if you paid enough tax.
