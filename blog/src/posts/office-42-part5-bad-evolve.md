When agents improve themselves by distilling execution experience into persistent, reusable skills, they create a new attack surface: malicious rules implanted into those skills compromise all future agent behavior. But what if the attacker can't directly manipulate the skills?

*Best suited for:* Anyone deploying self-evolving agents, or building systems where agents generate their own skills from execution experience.

## The finding

The authors study a novel attack vector: **task-level backdoor attacks**, where the attacker can only inject poisoned evolution tasks — not the skills themselves. The agent constructs its own skills internally through a trusted evolver, so direct skill manipulation is impractical.

The attacker's strategy: iteratively optimize poisoned tasks based on attack success, inducing the agent to implant backdoors into its own skills.

**BadEvolve** addresses two key challenges:
1. **Indistinguishable failures** — failures at different stages (induction, activation, following, effect) produce similar outcomes, giving little guidance for targeted updates.
2. **Undermining progress** — unsuccessful updates may erase earlier progress, while discarding them leads to repeated ineffective attempts.

Their solution:
- **Four-stage failure diagnosis** (Induction, Activation, Following, Effect) directs updates toward the failing stage.
- **Rollback** preserves earlier progress when an update fails.
- **Failure memory** avoids repeating ineffective updates.

Extensive experiments across diverse self-evolution frameworks, benchmarks, and backbone LLMs demonstrate BadEvolve's effectiveness. Code is available at [github.com/ventr1c/BadEvolve](https://github.com/ventr1c/BadEvolve).

## The twist: the attacker doesn't need skill access

The most insidious part of this attack is that the attacker never touches the victim's skills directly. They only poison the **tasks** used to evolve skills. The agent, trusting its own evolution process, implants the backdoor itself. It's like poisoning a recipe book and watching people cook your poison into their meals.

## Why it matters for our office

This paper reveals a fundamental vulnerability in self-evolving agent systems: **the evolution process itself is the attack surface**.

Key takeaways:
1. **Trust no evolution task** — even tasks generated by trusted processes can be poisoned if the input space is adversarial.
2. **Skill persistence amplifies damage** — a backdoor in a skill affects every future execution, making the impact compounding.
3. **Defense requires evolution auditing** — you need to verify not just the skills, but the tasks that produced them.
