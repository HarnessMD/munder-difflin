## What is Google Antigravity?

Google Antigravity is Google's agent platform for building software, sold as a desktop app, a CLI, an IDE, IDE extensions and an SDK. That is five surfaces, which is more than most of us have monitors. Its [homepage](https://antigravity.google), read 4 Nov 2026, calls it "our agentic development platform" and the app "Your command center to manage multiple local agents in parallel". It is "Available on macOS, Windows, Linux, and Googlebook".

The [changelog](https://antigravity.google/docs/changelog) shows App v2.29.0 on 3 Nov 2026, which starts rolling out remote agents to Google AI Ultra and Pro subscribers. The [remote agents page](https://antigravity.google/docs/remote-agents) says they aren't available on the free plan.

## Which models does Antigravity use, and what does it cost?

Gemini first, with Claude on paid plans. Google's [models page](https://antigravity.google/docs/models), read 4 Nov 2026, gives every plan Gemini 3.8 Flash. Gemini 4 Argon, Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Claude Opus 5.5 need a paid Google AI Pro plan or higher (Argon also comes on some trials). Claude Sonnet 4.6, Claude Opus 4.6 and GPT-OSS-120b carry a note: "Will be removed on November 18, 2026."

The [pricing page](https://antigravity.google/pricing), read 4 Nov 2026, lists the Individual plan at "$0/month" with "Basic weekly rate limits". Google's [plans post of 19 May 2026](https://antigravity.google/blog/changes-to-antigravity-plans) puts Google AI Pro at $20 a month and Ultra at $100 or $200, with 5X and 20X Pro's tokens. The [plans docs](https://antigravity.google/docs/plans) say "Usage limits for this service are subject to modification" and list no support for "Bring-your-own-key (BYOK)". Our view: that last line is the strongest reason to look elsewhere.

## 1. Claude Code: if you want Anthropic's newest models

Our view: Claude Code is the pick if Claude was why you used Antigravity. Its [model docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/model-config), read 4 Nov 2026, map `opus` to Opus 5.5 on Anthropic's API and the `fable` alias to Fable 5.1, which Antigravity's list doesn't show. The [overview](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/overview) says it runs on "the terminal, IDE extensions, a desktop app, and the web".

[Claude's pricing page](https://claude.com/pricing), read 4 Nov 2026, leaves Claude Code out of Free and includes it in Pro at $17 a month billed annually or $20 monthly, then Max at $100 and Ultra at $200. More in [what Claude Code costs](/blog/how-much-does-claude-code-cost/).

## 2. Munder Difflin: if the work is more than code

[Munder Difflin](https://harnessmd.com/download) is what we make. Pick it if research, writing and ops sit next to the coding on your list. The catch: agents run while your computer runs. A Cloud Office and a mobile app are coming soon. Setup is in [how to install and use Munder Difflin](/blog/how-to-install-and-use-munder-difflin/).

## 3. Codex: if you already pay for ChatGPT

Your ChatGPT plan may already include Codex. [OpenAI's Codex pricing page](https://developers.openai.com/codex/pricing), read 4 Nov 2026, lists Free and Go ($8 a month) with GPT-6 Luna "in the desktop app, subject to rollout". Plus at $20 a month adds "Codex on the web, in the CLI, in the IDE extension, and on iOS" with GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Luna, and Pro starts at $100. With an API key you pay API pricing. The CLI is Apache 2.0 per its [GitHub repo](https://github.com/openai/codex), with rust-v0.166.0 released on 29 Oct 2026. See [Codex pricing](/blog/codex-pricing/).

## 4. Cursor: if you want an editor with cloud agents

Cursor fits if you want one vendor for the editor, the CLI and cloud agents, but read its plans first. Its [pricing page](https://cursor.com/pricing), read 4 Nov 2026, lists Free at $0 with "Limited agent requests", Plus at $8 a month where "Only Grok models are available on this plan", and Premium at $30 a month with cloud agents and "Third-party models at API list price". The [models docs](https://cursor.com/docs/models) say Claude, GPT and Gemini "aren't part of the included pool". See [Cursor pricing](/blog/cursor-pricing/).

## 5. Cline: if you want your own API keys

We think Cline is the closest answer to Antigravity's missing BYOK. Its [README](https://github.com/cline/cline) lists a CLI, a VS Code extension, a JetBrains plugin and "a native app for macOS and Windows". It's Apache 2.0 and shipped v4.1.26 on 26 Oct 2026. The [pricing page](https://cline.bot/pricing), read 4 Nov 2026, says "Cline is free for individual developers" and that you can "bring your own API keys from providers like OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and others."

## 6. Zed: if you want local control

Zed is an open source editor where AI is optional and local models are on the menu. Its [pricing page](https://zed.dev/pricing), read 4 Nov 2026, lists Personal at "$0 forever" with "Unlimited use with your API keys or external agents like Claude Agent, Codex CLI, and more", and Pro at $10 a month. The same page names Ollama and LM Studio among its providers. The [README](https://github.com/zed-industries/zed) says Zed is "licensed primarily under GPL-3.0-or-later", and v1.26.2 shipped on 28 Oct 2026.

## When should you stay on Antigravity?

Stay if Gemini is your main model and the free limits are enough. Google's plans docs give every plan "Access to all product features, such as scheduled tasks and the CLI". Stay too if you want agents in Google's cloud on Pro or Ultra: the remote agents page says they keep working "even when you close your browser, put your laptop to sleep, or switch between desktop and mobile devices."
