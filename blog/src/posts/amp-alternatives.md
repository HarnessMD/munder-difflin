## What is Amp?

Amp is a coding agent with its own cloud machines. Its [manual](https://ampcode.com/manual), read 19 Nov 2026, opens: "Amp is a coding agent and development environment built for the frontier." It runs agents in orbs, and the [pricing page](https://ampcode.com/pricing) explains: "An orb is a cloud computer where Amp can work remotely." The manual's pitch is "Send a prompt, close your laptop, and the agent keeps working." You can also run `amp` in your terminal, and the [home page](https://ampcode.com/) lists apps for Mac, iPhone, Android, Windows and Linux. Its [news feed](https://ampcode.com/news) shows "Amp for Windows" on 17 Nov 2026. Its [About page](https://ampcode.com/about) says "We are an independent agent research lab".

## How much does Amp cost?

Amp is free to start, and paid tiers buy cloud time and credits. On 19 Nov 2026 the [pricing page](https://ampcode.com/pricing) said "Hobby has no monthly fee, and you can use your own model subscription or key and buy Amp credits as needed." It listed Megawatt at $20 a month with "45,000 minutes of Amp-hosted orbs".

## What changed in Amp on 12 Nov 2026?

Amp dropped its Dial for a model selector. The [12 Nov 2026 post](https://ampcode.com/news/bye-bye-dial) says goodbye to "the Dial and its four modes: low, medium, high, and ultra." You now pick the model for each thread. New threads start on Staff Pick, "which is Claude Opus 5.5 today." The post's own subheading is "Yes, We Changed Our Mind", which is at least honest.

## Why look for an Amp alternative?

Our view: pace of change is the reason the sources back. Amp's About page says: "If you're looking for a product that will stay the same and check every procurement box, this isn't it." The manual adds "No backward compatibility, no legacy features." The Dial lasted from July to November, and the 12 Nov post says "Dial settings don't carry over." Otherwise, read this as tools like Amp to compare before you pick.

## 1. Claude Code: the closest match to Amp

Claude Code is the nearest fit: one agent that works in your terminal and your editor. Anthropic's [README](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code), read 19 Nov 2026, calls it "an agentic coding tool that lives in your terminal, understands your codebase, and helps you code faster" and says "Use it in your terminal, IDE, or tag `@claude` on Github." Its [docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/overview) say "Most surfaces require a Claude subscription or Anthropic Console account." [v2.1.328](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/releases/tag/v2.1.328) came out on 19 Nov 2026. We think it suits people who already pay for Claude and want Amp's terminal flow. More in [Claude Code alternatives](/blog/claude-code-alternatives/).

## 2. Munder Difflin: a team of agents for more than code

Munder Difflin is the pick when you want a team of agents rather than one coding agent. Its [README](https://github.com/HarnessMD/munder-difflin), read 19 Nov 2026, says it "Wraps Claude Code, Antigravity (Gemini), OpenAI Codex" and names Gemini CLI, OpenCode and Cursor in the same list. So engines like those become one part of larger work. Agents take work from Slack or a webhook and use MCP servers, skills and plugins. The Cloud Office runs your agents in the cloud, always on, so work goes on with your computer off. We think it suits mixed work: research, writing and code. Setup: [how to install and use Munder Difflin](/blog/how-to-install-and-use-munder-difflin/).

## 3. Codex CLI: OpenAI's agent on your computer

Codex CLI suits people who already pay for ChatGPT. Its [README](https://github.com/openai/codex), read 19 Nov 2026, says "Codex CLI is a coding agent from OpenAI that runs locally on your computer". It recommends signing in "to use Codex as part of your Plus, Pro, Business, Edu, or Enterprise plan", and an API key works too. It is Apache 2.0 licensed, and [rust-v0.170.0](https://github.com/openai/codex/releases/tag/rust-v0.170.0) came out on 18 Nov 2026. We think it fits if you want OpenAI's own harness rather than Amp's. More in [Codex alternatives](/blog/codex-alternatives/).

## 4. OpenCode: the open source option

OpenCode is the pick if you want your agent's code in the open. Its [README](https://github.com/anomalyco/opencode), read 19 Nov 2026, says "The open source AI coding agent." It is MIT licensed, has two built in agents, build and plan, that you switch with the `Tab` key. [v2.3.2](https://github.com/anomalyco/opencode/releases/tag/v2.3.2) came out on 18 Nov 2026. We think it suits developers who want to patch their own agent.

## 5. Cursor: agents in an editor, on the web and on iOS

Cursor suits people who want agents inside a full editor. Its [home page](https://cursor.com/), read 19 Nov 2026, says "Cursor is your coding agent for building ambitious software." Its newest [changelog](https://cursor.com/changelog) entry, 6 Nov 2026, adds voice chat with agents, which "works in the Agents Window, the IDE, on web, and in Cursor for iOS." On 19 Nov 2026 its [pricing page](https://cursor.com/pricing) ran from Free at $0 a month, with "Limited agent requests", to Ultra at $200 a month. We think it suits people who live in an editor.

## How to choose

Pick by where the agent should live and who pays for the model. Our view:

- Terminal and IDE, on a Claude plan: Claude Code.
- Team of agents for all kinds of work, on your computer or in the Cloud Office: Munder Difflin.
- ChatGPT plan: Codex CLI.
- Open source: OpenCode.
- Editor: Cursor.
- Orbs, phone apps and fast change suit you: stay on Amp.
