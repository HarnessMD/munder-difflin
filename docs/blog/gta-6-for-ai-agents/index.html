I Built a GTA 6 Style City for My Claude Code and Codex Agents. They Work While I Play the Real GTA 6

On GTA 6 launch night, I gave my AI agent office one request: 21 posts and a sunny caper crew office. Here is the overnight playbook.

CG Chaitanya Giri · Nov 19, 2026 · 5 min read Story

On GTA 6 launch night, I gave my office of AI agents one request: 21 posts and a GTA 6 style office for the agents to work in. Then I logged off. Today’s plan: I play the real game, the office keeps going, and I approve what ships. Checked 19 Nov 2026.

Rockstar’s GTA VI page reads “Coming November 19, 2026”, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. I build Munder Difflin, free and open source: a desktop app that runs a team of AI agents for all kinds of knowledge work, coding included, on your own computer. Launch guides: our GTA 6 hub.

What did I ask my agents for? #

One request, with the launch as the deadline. In the small hours of 19 Nov 2026, India time, I asked for 20 GTA 6 posts for people searching about the launch, plus this one, motion graphics in every post, and a GTA 6 style office simulation for my agents.

Give or take a paragraph about quality, that was the prompt. The structure did the rest.

How did the agents split the work? #

A planner first, then one job per helper. My office’s SEO agent pulled 612 real Google autocomplete phrasings, mapped 112 keywords across 21 posts, wrote a fact pack of sources and briefed the helpers.

Agent (launch night, 19 Nov 2026) How many One job
SEO agent 1 Plan, keywords, fact pack, briefs
Writer 1 per post Writes from its row and the fact pack
Fact checker 1 per 2 posts Reads every source again, fixes the post
Animation builder 1 per post Three animations and a title card, in code
Temp 1 Builds the office simulation

The helpers ran on Claude Code, which Anthropic’s docs call “an agentic coding tool that reads your codebase, edits files, runs commands”.

One hiccup: a Codex helper never started. It was shut down after 20 idle minutes and its job went to another agent. Every office has one who misses the first morning. Watch for no shows, not only failures.

How do you hand a big overnight job to an agent office? #

Write one request, then let files do the remembering.

  1. One request with a deadline. What, how many, by when.
  2. A plan file. One row per piece in PLAN.md: slug, title, keywords, audience, style. Writers read their row, not your mind.
  3. A shared fact pack. SOURCES.md holds each fact, its link, the exact words and the date read. Writers quote it instead of guessing.
  4. One writer per piece. We think small jobs finish, and one agent juggling 21 posts does not.
  5. A separate fact checker. It reads every source again and fixes the post. Nobody grades their own homework.
  6. Small visual builders, one style each. Mine: split flap boards, radar sweeps, Art Deco posters, comic panels and more.
  7. Commits as work lands. A stalled helper then costs one piece, not the night.
  8. A human approval at the end. A pull request you review. Nothing goes out on an agent’s word.

The orchestrator, the task board, temps and automations are part of what harnessmd.com calls “the Pro workspace”, and “The free plan lets you use Pro features up to a monthly allowance of work tokens.” Before a first night run: overnight AI coding, realistically.

What does a GTA 6 style office for agents look like? #

Sunny, neon and original. My agents are the Palmira Beach Crew, a beach town caper crew whose hideout is The Palm Court Motel, an Art Deco beachfront motel in Palmira Beach.

The rooms: a lobby of desks under ceiling fans; a corkboard planning wall (the task board); a diner booth with a jukebox (the one to one spot); a pool deck; a garage with a convertible on a lift; a marina dock; an arcade corner.

No character, name, place, logo or art from GTA 6 or any Rockstar game is in it, renamed or not. “GTA 6 style” means the mood of the genre: sun, neon and a caper crew. No weapons, no police. The heists are work tasks.

A temp agent built it with the app’s simulation builder. Codex’s own image generation drew every picture: the room, the furniture, nine characters and the cover. Code only cut, shrank, cleaned colours and packed it. It passed the app’s own check and sits in my simulations, ready to pick in the Theme panel on Home.

Who is in the crew? #

Nine characters, one Boss.

Rosa Vela is the Boss, the orchestrator at seat 0. With her: Dex Marlow, the wheelman; Juno Park, the hacker; Kai Moreno, the influencer; Benny Shoal, the fixer; Tess Gale, the lookout; Ollie Finch, the numbers man; Marisol Reyes, the airboat captain; Nia Brooks, the gearhead.

Finished work is a sealed manila envelope marked DONE, carried to the Boss, and a finished task sets off a burst of yellow, aqua and lilac stars. Breaks are coconuts by the pool. Between jobs they tune the convertible, check the dock or run a walkie talkie check.

Why bother with a custom office theme? #

Our view: state you can see beats state you have to query. I like that one glance shows who is working, who is on a break and what is done: desk, coconut, envelope. A log says the same thing at far lower coconut density.

What happens on launch day? #

My plan for today: play the real game while the office keeps going, then review the branch and approve what ships. A plan, not a result. If your laptop has to sleep while you are playing, the Cloud Office is live: agents in the cloud, always on, and work goes on with your computer off.
