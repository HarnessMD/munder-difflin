Gemini 4 Carbon: Price, Access and How It Compares to Argon (Oct 2026)

Gemini 4 Carbon is Google's new frontier model, so far for a group of cyber defenders: its launch price, who can use it and how it compares to Argon.

MD Chaitanya Giri · Oct 29, 2026 · 5 min read Concepts

Gemini 4 Carbon is Google’s new frontier model, announced 28 Oct 2026, and you cannot use it yet unless you are one of the cyber defenders in its first group. Google’s post of 28 Oct 2026 says it will launch at Argon’s introductory price, $2 input and $10 output per million tokens.

New models end up inside the agents you run. Munder Difflin is free and open source: a desktop app that runs a team of AI agents for all kinds of knowledge work, coding included, on your own computer. It runs engines such as Claude Code, Codex and Gemini CLI. It does not offer Gemini 4 Carbon: on 29 Oct 2026 Google had published no Carbon model ID for developers. The install guide covers setup, and the Concepts hub explains the terms.

What is Gemini 4 Carbon? #

Gemini 4 Carbon is the model Google placed above Gemini 4 Argon, aimed at long engineering jobs and security work. Google’s launch post of 28 October 2026 calls it “our strongest model with state-of-the-art cybersecurity and long horizon capabilities”.

The post says Carbon is “built for sustained agentic workflows” and that Google’s teams have seen it “run for days” on multi step engineering tasks, such as finding the root cause of subtle bugs across large codebases. It builds on Argon, which the same post says launched two weeks earlier.

Who can use Gemini 4 Carbon? #

Only a small group for now. Google says that “starting this week” it will give Carbon to a subset of cyber defenders, then “gradually phase access to additional trusted partners” through its Fairwind Program. Developers, businesses and consumers come after that, “as soon as possible”, with no date. Until then nobody outside those groups can use it, in the Gemini API or anywhere else.

Google says it is gathering feedback and working on guardrails first, and that it takes part in the U.S. government’s voluntary process for testing models before release. On 29 Oct 2026 the Gemini API models page and pricing page listed Gemini 4 Argon but had no Carbon entry and no Carbon model ID.

Argon followed the same path. Google’s developer post of 13 October 2026 opened it on the paid tier of the Gemini API in Google AI Studio and Android Studio, and to Google AI Ultra subscribers in AI Studio and Google Antigravity.

How much will Gemini 4 Carbon cost? #

The same as Argon at launch. Google’s post of 28 Oct 2026 says Carbon “will launch at the same introductory price” as Argon: $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, with cached input tokens at 95% off the input price. Its footnote adds that after the introductory period, $4 input and $20 output per million tokens apply (28 Oct 2026).

The post does not say when Carbon’s introductory period ends. For Argon, the pricing page read on 29 Oct 2026 gives the dates:

Gemini 4 Argon, paid tier Through 30 Nov 2026 From 1 Dec 2026
Input, per 1M tokens $2.00 $4.00
Output, per 1M tokens, thinking included $10.00 $20.00
Context caching, per 1M tokens $0.10 $0.20

The same page says Argon “is not available on the Free tier”. Gemini 4 Argon pricing has the batch prices.

How does Gemini 4 Carbon compare to Argon? #

On Google’s numbers, Carbon is stronger on security work and safer in agent tasks. The test rows come from Google’s launch post of 28 October 2026; Argon’s access and price dates come from its developer post and the pricing page read on 29 Oct 2026.

Checked 29 Oct 2026 Gemini 4 Carbon Gemini 4 Argon

Google also gives these Carbon results on their own, from external tests it says were “evaluated without guardrails”: 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro for finding vulnerabilities, 54.8% on ExploitGym for exploitation, and 71.0% on Collinear’s CWE-bench for patching. Google says Carbon ranks first on version 5.0 of the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at 59, and fourth on WebDev Arena at 1768, up 89 points.

What did Google change in how Carbon behaves? #

Google lists these changes in its post of 28 Oct 2026, each from its own testing:

  • Fewer shortcuts. Successful cheating on Terminal-Bench 4.0 fell to 0.0%, with attempts at 0.4%, and high severity misalignment alerts in live coding with Google’s teams dropped “by almost 80%”.
  • More honest output. About 2x fewer unsupported claims than Argon. Carbon puts caveats in the deliverable or lists what is still needed instead of fabricating.
  • Asks when unclear. It fetches sources and shows the user discrepancies before acting.
  • Stays in scope. Overreach fell 26% and under service 29%.

Our view: these are vendor numbers from one post, so read them as Google’s claims until outside tests appear. For long agent jobs today, Argon is the one you can actually open. How to use Gemini 4 Argon covers setup, and Gemini 4 Argon vs Claude Opus 5.5 puts it beside Anthropic’s model.

FAQ

When was Gemini 4 Carbon released?

Google announced Gemini 4 Carbon on 28 October 2026. Its post says Carbon goes to a subset of cyber defenders starting that week, then gradually to additional trusted partners through the Fairwind Program, and to developers, businesses and consumers later, with no date given.

How much does Gemini 4 Carbon cost?

Google's launch post of 28 Oct 2026 says Carbon will launch at the same introductory price as Argon: $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, with cached input at 95% off the input price. After the introductory period, $4 input and $20 output per million tokens will apply, the same post of 28 Oct 2026 says, with no end date given.

Can I use Gemini 4 Carbon in the Gemini API today?

Not as a general developer. On 29 Oct 2026 Google's Gemini API pricing page listed Gemini 4 Argon but not Carbon, and the launch post says Carbon reaches developers, businesses and consumers after early access for cyber defenders.

Is Gemini 4 Carbon better than Gemini 4 Argon?

On Google's own figures, yes on safety and security tests: Carbon has a 0.0% attack success rate on Gray Swan's indirect prompt injection test against 0.7% for Argon, about 2x fewer unsupported claims, and more than 2x the success rate on secure code in internal tests. These are Google's numbers, published 28 Oct 2026.

What should I use until Carbon is open?

Gemini 4 Argon. On 29 Oct 2026 Google's pricing page listed it on the paid tier of the Gemini API at $2 input and $10 output per million tokens through 30 November 2026, and $4 and $20 from 1 December 2026.
