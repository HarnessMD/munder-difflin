Meta Muse Alternatives: 7 Picks If Muse Isn't in Your Country or You Want Your Data Back (Oct 2026)

Meta Muse is in the US and Canada and trains on your chats by default. Seven alternatives, open source and hosted, with access and plans checked 30 Oct 2026.

CG Chaitanya Giri · Sep 26, 2026 · 4 min read Comparisons

Our view: the best Meta Muse alternative depends on why you are leaving. Outside Muse’s countries, start with OpenClaw, an open source assistant on your own computer. For a team of agents on your work, try Munder Difflin. For a hosted agent, Grok Bot, Gemini tasks and Claude Cowork are closest. Checked 30 Oct 2026.

Munder Difflin is free and open source: a desktop app that runs a team of AI agents for all kinds of knowledge work, coding included, on your own computer. This page sits in our Comparisons hub.

Why look for a Meta Muse alternative? #

Three reasons come up most.

  • Muse is not in your country. Meta’s launch post of 8 Sep 2026 says Muse “is rolling out in the US”. iPhone in Canada reported it reached Canada on 18 Sep, and RuntimeWire reported that a Meta executive said it was launching in Japan on 23 Oct.
  • You don’t want Meta training on your chats. Meta’s help page, read 30 Oct 2026, says the training setting “is on when you first use Muse”. You can switch it off under Settings, Data controls.
  • You want agents for your work. Meta’s launch post gives sending an email or booking travel as examples of what Muse does. For Meta’s coding agent, see Muse Code vs Claude Code.

1. OpenClaw: the closest thing to Muse that you own #

OpenClaw is “an open-source AI assistant that runs on your own computer”, MIT licensed, with more than 390,000 stars on 30 Oct 2026. It replies in Discord, iMessage, Slack, Teams, Telegram, WhatsApp “and 20+ more”. The catch, per its README: tools run on the host “unless you configure sandboxing”, so set that up first.

2. Munder Difflin: a team of agents for your work #

Munder Difflin is what we make. It runs a team of agents for research, writing, ops and coding on engines such as Claude Code, Codex and Gemini CLI. Each agent has its own memory and inbox, and they share a task board. Work comes in from Slack and webhooks, and tools connect through MCP servers, skills and plugins. A Cloud Office and a mobile app are coming soon. The catch: agents run while your computer runs.

3. Grok Bot: hosted bots that keep working #

Grok Bot’s plans page, read 30 Oct 2026, says it is included on Premium, Super and Ultra in The X Subscription, and on paid legacy Cursor plans. Its FAQ says every Bot on an account shares “one persistent cloud computer”, and closing your laptop does not stop a background turn. See Meta Muse vs Grok Bot.

4. Gemini tasks: for people who live in Google #

Google renamed Gemini Spark. Its help page, read 30 Oct 2026, says: “we renamed it Task to make it easier to understand.” Tasks need a Google AI Pro or Ultra plan, a personal Google Account and an age of 18 or over, Keep Activity on and Personal Intelligence turned on, and for now are not available in the European Economic Area, Nigeria, Switzerland or the United Kingdom.

5. Claude Cowork: for documents, files and research #

Anthropic’s Cowork guide, read 30 Oct 2026, lists it on the paid Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise plans. It “runs your sessions remotely in the cloud”, keeps working when you close your laptop, and on desktop can read and write your local files.

6. Hermes Agent: for memory that grows #

Hermes Agent from Nous Research is MIT licensed. Its README says it creates skills from experience, curates its own memory and talks over Telegram, Discord, Slack, WhatsApp and Signal. You pick the model provider and can switch with hermes model.

7. OpenMausBot: a chat app of local bots #

OpenMausBot calls itself “The open-source Grok Bot alternative”, and its README also names it an alternative to Meta Muse. Each bot is Claude or Codex running locally, with its own model and connected apps. Apache 2.0, and on GitHub trending on 30 Oct 2026.

Two more to watch: Instinct, a texting agent still in private beta, and OpenAI’s dots, covered in ChatGPT dots alternatives.

How do you choose? #

Start from the reason you are leaving.

  • Muse is not in your country. OpenClaw or Hermes Agent, both open source on your own hardware.
  • You don’t want your chats used for training. An open source pick with a model provider you choose, and read that provider’s terms.
  • You want agents for your work. Claude Cowork if you prefer hosted, Munder Difflin for a team on your computer, or Gemini tasks if you live in Google.

FAQ

Why can't I use Meta Muse?

Muse launched in the US on 8 September 2026, and iPhone in Canada reported it reached Canada on 18 September. RuntimeWire reported on 23 October that a Meta executive said it was launching in Japan. Meta has not published a full country list or dates for other countries.

Is there an open source alternative to Meta Muse?

Yes. OpenClaw is the closest: an open source assistant, MIT licensed, that runs on your own computer and replies in WhatsApp, iMessage, Telegram, Slack and more than 20 other channels. Munder Difflin is a free and open source desktop app for a team of agents, and Hermes Agent and OpenMausBot are open source too.

Does Meta Muse train on my data?

By default, yes. Meta's help page, read 30 Oct 2026, says the setting that lets Meta use your Muse interactions to improve its models is on when you first use Muse, and you can turn it off in Settings under Data controls. Meta says Muse does not share your conversations or VM data with its ad systems.

What is the best free alternative to Meta Muse?

OpenClaw, Munder Difflin, Hermes Agent and OpenMausBot are free and open source. With OpenClaw, Hermes Agent and OpenMausBot you pay only for the AI model you connect; Munder Difflin uses engines such as Claude Code, Codex and Gemini CLI. Checked 30 Oct 2026.
