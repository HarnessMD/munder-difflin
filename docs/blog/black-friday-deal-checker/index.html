Black Friday 2026: Spot Fake Deals With an AI Agent Team

Check the price history, not the was price. Black Friday 2026 dates, FTC pricing rules, holiday return dates, Target price match and a checklist.

CG Chaitanya Giri · Nov 26, 2026 · 5 min read Use Cases

A Black Friday deal is real when today’s price beats the item’s own price history; a fake one only beats the “was” price on its tag. Check the history, the return date and the price match rule before you pay, and you buy what you planned at a real low price. Store pages checked 25 Nov 2026.

When are Black Friday and Cyber Monday 2026? #

Black Friday is Friday, 27 Nov 2026. timeanddate.com, checked 25 Nov 2026, lists Thanksgiving Day on 26 Nov 2026 and Cyber Monday on 30 Nov 2026.

How do you tell if a Black Friday deal is real? #

Compare today’s price with what the item really sold for. The FTC’s Guides Against Deceptive Pricing, 16 CFR Part 233, read 25 Nov 2026, say a former price “provides a legitimate basis for the advertising of a price comparison” when it is “the actual, bona fide price at which the article was offered to the public on a regular basis for a reasonably substantial period of time”. When the former price is fictitious, “the “reduced” price is, in reality, probably just the seller’s regular price.”

For a price said to be charged elsewhere, section 233.2 says “the advertised higher price must be based upon fact, and not be fictitious or misleading.” We think a tag that says Was next to Now proves nothing by itself.

How do you see an item’s price history? #

Look it up before the sale page tells you what to think, and set an alert for a drop. From each tool’s own page, read 25 Nov 2026:

  • Keepa, for Amazon, describes itself as “Amazon price history charts, price drop alerts, price watches, daily drops and browser extensions.”
  • Google’s price tracking help page says “You can track the price of a product using the Track Price feature.” You can “Optionally, select a target price”, and “When the price drops below your target price” you get a phone notification and an email. “Price tracking is available in the United States, Canada, Australia, Japan, and India.”

Our view: note the lowest price seen, with site and date; that is the price to beat.

How long can you return Black Friday buys? #

Into January at both stores below, but the dates differ. From each store’s own page, checked 25 Nov 2026:

  • Amazon (return policy): “most items purchased between November 1 and December 31, 2026 can be returned through January 31, 2027.”
  • Target (holiday returns): qualifying purchases “made between November 1 and December 24, 2026” get extra time. Electronics and entertainment, excluding Apple and Beats, “must be returned by January 24, 2027”; Apple, Beats and unlocked or prepaid mobile phones “must be returned by January 8, 2027.”

Will a store match a lower price after you buy? #

At Target you can ask, for its own lower price. Its Price Match Guarantee, read 25 Nov 2026, says: “Starting November 1, 2026, if you purchase an item in store or online and the price goes lower at Target on or before December 24, 2026, you can request a price match.” The lower price “must be found at Target.com or in a local Target store”, and “Screenshots or pictures cannot be accepted.”

What goes on your fake deal checklist? #

Five checks, each from a page above:

  1. Price history. Is today’s price below the lowest price seen? For Amazon, Keepa shows price history charts.
  2. Former price. Was the “was” price offered “for a reasonably substantial period of time”? If you cannot tell, ignore it.
  3. Return by. Write the date first: Amazon January 31, 2027; Target electronics January 24, 2027.
  4. Price match. At Target, ask for a match if Target’s price drops on or before December 24, 2026.
  5. Most I will pay. Above your own number, it is not your deal, whatever the tag says.

What does the agent team do? #

Five agents read public pages and write notes; you sign in, you pay and you press buy. No agent signs in to a store, holds your card or places an order.

  • Wishlist Keeper keeps the table above.
  • Price Historian finds the lowest price seen.
  • Policy Reader quotes each store’s return and price match rules.
  • Deal Watcher rechecks prices every few hours.
  • Budget Keeper totals the list and writes buy or wait.
Wishlist Keeper: Turn my notes into the wishlist table. Ask me for any missing number.
Price Historian: Find each item's lowest price seen on public pages, with site and date.
Policy Reader: Quote each store's return date and price match rule from its own page, with the link.
Deal Watcher: Every few hours until Cyber Monday, check each price. Message me when it hits my number.
Budget Keeper: Total the list, flag anything over my number, write buy or wait with one reason.

You get back the filled table and a short buy or wait note per item. We think the Budget Keeper earns its seat by Saturday.
