CrewAI Alternatives: 6 Picks for Teams of Agents (Nov 2026)

CrewAI is a free MIT framework with a cloud from Basic to Enterprise. Six CrewAI alternatives by licence, language and release, checked 24 Nov 2026.

MD Chaitanya Giri · Nov 24, 2026 · 5 min read Comparisons

Our view: LangGraph is the best CrewAI alternative for Python, and Mastra is the best for TypeScript. People look elsewhere for another language, lower level control, or a team of agents without writing code. CrewAI is a Python framework, and its cloud runs from a free Basic plan to a Custom Enterprise plan, checked 24 Nov 2026.

We make Munder Difflin, which is free and open source: a desktop app that runs a team of AI agents for all kinds of knowledge work, coding included, on your own computer. More: open source AI agents, Herdr alternatives and CrewAI and AutoGen vs a local agent harness.

What is CrewAI? #

CrewAI is an open source Python framework for teams of AI agents. Its README, read 24 Nov 2026, says it gives developers “autonomous agent collaboration through Crews and precise, event-driven control through Flows”. Crews use “role-based AI agents”: the README example gives one agent the role “{topic} Senior Data Researcher” and another “{topic} Reporting Analyst”. It is MIT licensed with 61,226 stars on 24 Nov 2026, and 1.15.45 came out that day.

How much does CrewAI cost? #

The framework is free under MIT; the paid side is a build and runtime platform. On 24 Nov 2026 the pricing page listed Basic as “Free”, with “50 workflow executions/month”, and Enterprise as “Custom”, with deploys “on CrewAI cloud, your own VPC, or your own infrastructure”. The README calls its commercial side the CrewAI AMP Suite.

What are the best CrewAI alternatives? #

Six picks for building or running a team of agents, as of 24 Nov 2026: LangGraph, Munder Difflin, OpenAI Agents SDK, Google ADK, Mastra and Agno. Stars and releases are from GitHub that day.

1. LangGraph: low level control over state #

We think LangGraph suits teams that want to draw the graph themselves. Its README, read 24 Nov 2026, calls it “a low-level orchestration framework for building, managing, and deploying long-running, stateful agents”. Agents “persist through failures” and resume “from exactly where they left off”. It is MIT licensed, Python first with a JS version, with 45,362 stars; langgraph 1.2.24 came out on 20 Nov 2026. Deep Agents is “a higher-level package built on LangGraph”. LangGraph itself is free. For deploying, the README points to LangSmith, a separate service, whose pricing page listed Developer at “$0 / seat per month” and Plus at “$39 / seat per month”, each “then pay as you go”, on 24 Nov 2026.

2. Munder Difflin: a team of agents without framework code #

Munder Difflin is an app, not a framework: you do not write your agents in Python. Its README, read 24 Nov 2026, says “Use the subscriptions you already pay for to run an office of AI agents on your own computer.” and “You hire agents, give each one a role, a face and a model, and hand them work in plain words: research, writing, analysis, planning, support, operations, and code too.” It says “Each agent runs the real CLI you already use, signed in with your own plan”, and its engines include Claude Code, Codex and Gemini CLI. The Cloud Office runs your agents in the cloud, always on, so work goes on with your computer off. Setup: how to install and use Munder Difflin.

3. OpenAI Agents SDK: handoffs between agents #

The OpenAI Agents SDK README, read 24 Nov 2026, calls it “a lightweight yet powerful framework for building multi-agent workflows” that is “provider-agnostic”, supporting OpenAI’s APIs “as well as 100+ other LLMs”. Agents delegate through handoffs and “Agents as tools”. It is MIT licensed Python with 30,853 stars, and v0.26.0 came out on 24 Nov 2026.

4. Google ADK: built for Gemini, open to others #

Google’s Agent Development Kit is “An open-source, code-first Python framework for building, evaluating, and deploying sophisticated AI agents”, per its README read 24 Nov 2026. “While optimized for Gemini, ADK is model-agnostic, deployment-agnostic, and compatible with other frameworks.” It is Apache 2.0 with 22,342 stars; v2.15.0 came out on 19 Nov 2026.

5. Mastra: the TypeScript pick #

Mastra is “a framework for building AI-powered applications and agents with a modern TypeScript stack”, per its README, read 24 Nov 2026. Workflows chain steps with .then(), .branch() and .parallel(). Its licence file puts content under any directory named ee/ under the Mastra Enterprise Edition License, whose Enterprise Features “are not open source”, and says "Content outside of the above-mentioned directories or restrictions is available under the “Apache License 2.0"”. It has 29,786 stars, and @mastra/core@1.84.0 came out on 18 Nov 2026. The pricing page listed Free at $0, Starter at $25 and Team at $250 per workspace each month on 24 Nov 2026.

6. Agno: agents as services in your cloud #

Agno’s README, read 24 Nov 2026, says “Agno is a self-managing agent platform” and you can “Combine agents into teams and workflows” and “Run them as durable services on AgentOS”. It is Apache 2.0 Python with 43,284 stars; v3.2.1 came out on 17 Nov 2026. The pricing page said “The runtime is free and open source” and “Pro is $150/month for the live control plane” on 24 Nov 2026.

What happened to Microsoft AutoGen? #

Microsoft archived it, so it is not a pick here. The AutoGen README, read 24 Nov 2026, says “Project archived and no longer maintained” and “New users should start with Microsoft Agent Framework”. The last release, python-v0.7.5, came out on 30 Sep 2025.

How to choose #

Our view:

  • Control over state in Python: LangGraph.
  • A team of agents for any work, without framework code: Munder Difflin.
  • Handoffs and many model providers: OpenAI Agents SDK.
  • Gemini and Google Cloud: Google ADK.
  • TypeScript: Mastra.
  • Agents as services in your cloud: Agno.
  • Roles and Crews in Python: stay on CrewAI.
